Coastal dune lakes

CBAEP describes the lakes this way in the update:

  • Shallow, irregularly shaped, and continually changing.
  • Water level and salinity can vary greatly.
  • Inputs include the Gulf, tributaries, runoff, precipitation, and groundwater seepage.
  • Generally colored, slightly acidic, with low to medium nutrient levels.
  • Critical habitat, including a freshwater source for migratory species.
  • Globally rare and critically imperiled.

Monitoring overview

CBA and trained volunteers collect the samples. University of Florida LAKEWATCH analyzes nutrients and clarity. The record now covers more than 20 years.

Who

  • Samples are taken by CBA and by citizen scientists trained by CBA.
  • At Lake Powell, RMA is involved and LPCA collects the samples.
  • One-on-one training supports the citizen science program.

What

  • University of Florida LAKEWATCH measures nutrients and clarity.
  • Additional water-quality parameters are measured in the field with a multi-probe meter.
  • The program has more than 20 years of data.

Where

  • Several of the larger lakes are split into sections, including Western Northeast, Western, and Grayton.
  • 18 waterbodies in total.
  • Each has 3 to 4 stations, tied to specific GPS sites.

Parameters measured

CBA records a set of readings in the field. LAKEWATCH analyzes nutrients, chlorophyll, and water clarity in the lab.

CBA, in the field

  • Temperature
  • Dissolved oxygen (mg/L and percent saturation)
  • Specific conductivity
  • Salinity
  • pH
  • Turbidity

LAKEWATCH, lab analysis

  • Nutrients: total phosphorus and total nitrogen
  • Chlorophyll-a
  • Secchi vanishing point

Trophic state

Trophic state is the amount of biological productivity in a waterbody. CBAEP uses a Trophic State Index (TSI) derived from chlorophyll-a.

TSI below 40

Oligotrophic

Minimal biomass. Clear. Oxygen-rich.

Mesotrophic

Moderate biomass. Somewhat clear. Potential anoxia at depth in summer months.

TSI 50 to 70

Eutrophic

High algal biomass. Cloudy. Anoxia at depth. Potentially a sign of excess nutrient levels.

The 2025 update did not name lakes in this group.

TSI above 70

Hyper-eutrophic

Very murky. Almost perpetual anoxia at depth. Dense mats of algae. Likely impaired status.

The 2025 update did not name lakes in this group.

From the field

Volunteers and CBA staff noted what they saw on the water during 2025. The notes below follow the update.

  • Algae bloom on the lake, bright orange in color. Fuller, November 1, 2025.
  • Dead tarpon (about 2.5 feet) and flounder in the lake. Deer, January 29, 2025.
  • The outfall to the Gulf was deep and rapid flowing, mini rapids. Camp Creek, May 5, 2025.
  • Draper blew out more than 3 weeks ago and is still draining. The lake is 5 feet lower than it was a month ago. Draper, November 25, 2025.
  • Cool air and water temperatures after a winter storm with snow. Alligator, January 26, 2025.

Fuller Lake bloom

  • Blooms observed November 2025 to February 2026.
  • A sample was sent to the University of Florida.
  • Identified as Botryococcus, possibly B. braunii, and pollen.
  • No human health effects. Large blooms can cause negative effects to the ecosystem.
  • Bottom dissolved oxygen remained high during sampling.
